Sheet 00 · do this first

How the Meta account should be structured

Do not create a Facebook profile for AI Overflow

Personal profiles on Facebook must be real individuals. A profile registered as a company violates Meta's terms and gets disabled — often months later, with no warning and no reliable appeal. When a profile is disabled, every asset it admins dies with it: the Page, the ad account, the pixel and its history, the WhatsApp connections. Two founders sharing one login also trips Meta's security checks — two cities, two devices, one profile — and you get locked out mid-campaign.

The structure that survives

Personal profiles are only keys. The Business Manager is the door, and it owns everything behind it.

There is no Business Manager login. business.facebook.com authenticates you through a personal Facebook profile — there is no separate username and password for a business portfolio, and no way to create one. Every company running Meta ads reaches it through individual people's profiles; the portfolio layer is what makes that safe. The AI Overflow email sits inside the portfolio as its contact and billing address, and is never a credential.

  • Each founder logs in as themselves. Real name, real profile, no shared password. Both get Full control on the Business Manager.
  • The AI Overflow email goes on the Business Manager as the business contact and billing address — never as a profile login.
  • If a founder leaves or gets locked out, nothing is lost. Remove the person, the assets stay. That is the entire point of this structure.
  • Turn on required two-factor for everyone in Business Settings → Security Centre. A locked-out admin at ₹400/day is an expensive afternoon.

Order of operations

  • Each founder confirms they have a normal personal Facebook profile in their own real name, with 2FA on. If one doesn't, they create one — as themselves
  • Founder A opens business.facebook.com logged in as themselves and creates a business portfolio named AI Overflow
  • Inside it: Business Info → set the contact email to the AI Overflow address. This is a notification and billing field, not a credential
  • Add Founder B under People → Full control, using the email tied to their personal profile. They accept while logged in as themselves. Not "Employee access"
  • Create the SellOS Page inside the Business Manager — not from a personal profile, or it lands in the wrong place and migrating it later is painful
  • Create the ad account inside the Business Manager, and add AI Overflow's GSTIN to it
  • Connect both WhatsApp Business numbers under WhatsApp accounts
  • Add the company card, set the account spending limit to ₹5,000
  • Post 5–6 things on the Page before any ad runs

Three things that cost money if you skip them

  • GST is charged on top of ad spend, not inside it. ₹5,000 of delivery bills you roughly ₹5,900. Put AI Overflow's GSTIN on the ad account and that 18% becomes claimable input credit — about ₹900 back on this test alone. It cannot be added retrospectively to invoices already issued.
  • An empty Page kills the ad. A chemist who taps your ad will check who you are. Zero posts and four followers reads as a scam. Post the flyer content, a real bill, a photo of the anchor shop with their permission — six posts is enough, and it costs nothing.
  • Brand-new ad accounts get reviewed. Don't create the account and immediately push ₹400/day. Set the Page up, let it exist for a few days, then start. A restricted account on day one costs you the whole flight window.

One wrinkle the sequential flights already solve

Click-to-WhatsApp ads pick their destination number from the WhatsApp accounts in your Business Manager, but depending on how your Page is connected you may find the ad set only offers the number currently linked to the Page. Check the dropdown when you build the Tirupati ad set. If only one number appears, it costs you nothing: link the Tirupati number for days 1–7, swap to the Kolkata number for days 8–14. The flights never overlap, so a single Page-linked number is enough.

What Meta actually charges you for

You are not billed per click. On every objective that matters here, Meta bills per impression. The unqualified chemist who scrolled past your ad already cost you money. The one who tapped it cost you nothing extra.

ObjectiveBilled onNote

Video views ThruPlay Or 2-second continuous views. Not relevant to this test.

Why this changes the plan you described

  • You can't save money by repelling clicks. A deliberately off-putting creative just lowers CTR — and Meta charges a higher CPM for low-engagement ads. Over-filter and you pay more per qualified lead, not less.
  • The only free pre-click filter is the copy itself. Naming the audience and the price on the creative costs nothing and removes the wrong people before they tap.
  • Real qualification happens after the click, in WhatsApp, where every question you ask is free. That’s Sheet 08.
  • Your bottleneck is your own time, not impressions. Optimise for a clean inbox, not a low click count.
Sheet 02

Two cities on ₹5,000 — sequentially, not together

Meta needs roughly 50 optimisation events per ad set per week to leave the learning phase. At ₹167/day you never get there — you buy a month of erratic delivery and inflated costs, then a result that can't tell you whether the channel failed or the execution did. The same money at ₹350/day for 14 days exits learning, reads clean, and leaves you two weeks of the month to act on it.

Do not run both cities at once

₹5,000 split across two simultaneous ad sets is ₹175/day each — straight back into the starvation problem above, twice. And you can't solve it by putting both cities in one ad set either: Meta pours budget wherever conversations are cheapest, and Tirupati CPMs run a fraction of Kolkata's. You'd wake up on day four having spent 90% in Tirupati, learned nothing about Kolkata, and with no way to plan a field visit.

Run them as two flights — Tirupati first

Tirupati is the cheaper market by some distance, so it's where creative mistakes cost least. Burn your testing money at a ₹90 CPM, not a ₹280 one, then take the winning concept into Kolkata already knowing which hook works.

  • Flight 1 · Tirupati · days 1–7 · ₹300/day = ₹2,100. Creatives: Telugu ×2, English ×2.
  • Flight 2 · Kolkata · days 8–14 · ₹400/day = ₹2,800. Creatives: Bengali ×2, English ×2 — but drop whichever concept lost in Tirupati and run the winner in both languages.
  • Total ₹4,900, one ad set live at any moment, each exiting learning on its own budget.

Kill criteria — decide these now, not while it's running

  • ₹900 into a flight and CTR under 0.8% → the creative is wrong. Swap the losing concept. Don't touch targeting.
  • ₹1,400 into a flight and fewer than 10 conversations → targeting is too tight. Widen the radius and drop detailed targeting. In Tirupati, check the "living in this location" setting before you blame anything else.
  • Tirupati flight ends with zero qualified leads → do not launch Kolkata on the same creative. Rewrite the hook first, or you'll spend ₹2,800 repeating a known failure at triple the CPM.
  • Full ₹4,900 and zero booked setup slots → Facebook is not your channel at this stage. Go back to doors and don't retry until you have a case study.

Tirupati has a trap the other city doesn't

Tirupati carries an enormous floating pilgrim population, and a chunk of it is on Facebook inside your radius on any given day. Meta's default location setting is "People living in or recently in this location" — leave it there and you will pay to show a shop-software ad to devotees from three states who are here for two days.

  • Set location to "People living in this location". This is not optional in Tirupati.
  • Pin the shop clusters, not the temple. The chemists are around Ruia hospital, SVIMS, BIRRD and the bus stand / Gandhi Road stretch — not up the hill.
  • Expect noticeably lower CPMs than Kolkata. If Tirupati CPM comes back above Kolkata's, your location setting is wrong — check it before blaming the creative.

Detailed targeting — the honest position

Meta has no reliable "pharmacy owner" segment in India. What's available is weak: the Small business owners behaviour, and interests like Pharmacy or Pharmacist that mostly catch pharmacy students, not proprietors.

  • Layer Small business owners only, nothing else, and check the audience size.
  • If the estimate falls under ~50,000, remove it entirely. A tight radius plus an interest filter shrinks the pool below what ₹350/day can deliver against, and delivery costs spike.
  • Let the geography and the creative do the filtering. That is what the price on the artboard is for.

Sheet 05 · reference

Every pain we can actually claim

Only pains the shipped product genuinely addresses — no roadmap, no wishful thinking. A pain that makes a good doorstep question is not automatically a good ad hook, and the difference costs real money, so each one carries a verdict.

What makes a pain ad-worthy

  • He already knows he has it. A feed gives you three seconds. Anything needing education loses.
  • It has a number attached — rupees or hours. Vague discomfort doesn't stop a thumb.
  • Nobody is to blame. Pains he can blame on the notebook convert. Pains that imply he's careless don't.
  • He's not ashamed to be seen engaging with it. Facebook engagement is semi-public — his staff and neighbours can see it. This one rules out more good pains than people expect.

How to phrase the question

"Which of your medicines expire next month?" works because he cannot answer it, and the failing to answer is the pitch. Nothing is claimed, nothing is sold, and he arrives at the problem himself. Every question below is built to that shape:

  • Ten words or fewer. A long question gets answered with a shrug.
  • Name a real thing — a medicine, a month, yesterday, a box. Abstractions let him say "yes yes, it's fine."
  • Answerable in principle, unanswerable in his shop. That gap is the entire argument.
  • Never ask about a process. "How do you handle X" invites a defence. "How much X yesterday" invites a silence.

The two you must keep out of the ads

Staff theft is a top-three pain for every shop owner and a poison ad hook. Liking or commenting is visible, and no owner will publicly engage with an ad that accuses the boy behind his counter. Ask it at the doorstep, never in a feed.

"You can't see your shop when you're away" fails for the same reason in reverse — it advertises to his own staff that he's watching. It sells beautifully face to face and badly in public.

A timing trick the GST hook gives you for free

GSTR-1 is due on the 11th of each month. The GST pain is not felt evenly — it spikes hard in the five days before that date, when the CA is chasing and the owner is sitting with a calculator. If you run the GST hook, weight it to the 6th–11th. Same creative, same budget, a materially more receptive reader. No other pain on this list has a calendar attached to it.

Sheet 06 · 1080 × 1350 · 4:5

The four creatives

Three hooks × three languages, nine artboards. Only four run per flight: all three hooks in the local language, plus one English control. The hook is the variable you're testing — hold language constant so you can actually read the result. 4:5 is deliberate: it takes more vertical screen in feed than 1:1.

Why these are light, not dark

Your buyer is in his forties or fifties. Contrast sensitivity drops with age and the lens yellows, so light text on a dark ground blurs and haloes for exactly this reader — dark-on-light is measurably easier. He is also looking at his phone under shop tube lights or in daylight, where a dark creative loses contrast and a light one holds. And in Indian local-business advertising, dark high-design reads as "not for me"; clarity is the trust signal, not sophistication.

The trade-off is that a white creative can vanish into Facebook's white feed. That's what the solid accent footer is for — it bleeds edge to edge and gives the pattern interrupt without costing legibility. Type is also larger and heavier than a default creative for the same reason.

Each artboard carries a real screen from the app, cropped to the one thing the headline asks about — the expiring-soon rows, the live stock count, the bill in a chat. It replaces the explanatory sub-line, because showing the answer beats describing it. The surface is phone-proportioned on purpose: you sell "runs on the phone you already have", and a laptop would say the opposite. Nothing in it is smaller than 21px on the artboard, so it survives the shrink to feed size.

One claim you can't make yet

"All-in-one" now covers stock, distributors, customers and billing — all four shipped and demonstrable. It deliberately leaves out employee management: there are no staff logins, no roles and no per-user audit trail in the product today, single login per shop. Advertising it would break trust at the first demo, which is the one place you cannot afford to lose it, and it draws leads whose first question you'd have to answer with "not yet".

Policy note

Keep every claim about billing and business, never about medicines or health. Meta's reviewers apply health-related restrictions aggressively to anything that reads as pharmaceutical. You're selling shop software — the copy above stays on the right side of that line, so don't add anything about medicine efficacy, patients, or treatment.

What each answer means

GO

Chemist · copy or nothing · 1–2 counters

Your buyer. Book a setup slot immediately and hand them to Sheet 06 of the field kit. Don't sell further — you've already won, now deliver.

ASK

Chemist · Marg or other software · 1–2 counters

One question only: "Is it working well for you?" If yes, thank them and stop. If they're paying an AMC for something they've abandoned, that's a live lead.

STOP

Three or more counters

Out of scope — no staff logins, no roles, no audit trail. Say so plainly and ask if they know a smaller shop. A clean no earns you a referral more often than a stretch earns you a sale.

HOLD

Not a chemist — grocery or general store

The product covers them and the landing page says so, but they're not who this test is measuring. Answer warmly, log them separately, and don't let them distort your read on the campaign.

DROP

No reply within 24 hours

One follow-up, then stop. Chasing silent ad leads is how a ₹5,000 test quietly eats a month of your attention.

Where the demo video goes

Send it after they answer the three questions, never before. Two reasons: an unanswered "here's a video" ends the conversation, whereas the video handed over as a reward for answering keeps it alive and gives you a natural second message. And with the counter filter gone from the creative, those three answers are now doing all of your screening.

  • Under 90 seconds. A shop owner will not watch five minutes standing at his counter. One bill made start to finish, the expiry list, the GST export. Nothing else.
  • Shot on a phone, in a real shop. A screen recording of the app on a laptop undoes the "runs on your phone" promise the ad just made.
  • Three versions — English, Bengali, Telugu. Same footage, three voiceovers. It costs one afternoon and it's reusable forever.
  • Always follow it with a question, never let it sit: "Shall I come Tuesday and set it up?" A video with no ask is a dead end.

Reply speed is the whole game. A chemist who messaged from an ad is at their counter with their phone in hand. Answer inside ten minutes and they're still there; answer in three hours and you're a stranger texting them at dinner. If nobody can watch the inbox during shop hours, don't start the campaign yet.

The live blocker was not the flyer — it was landing/app/layout.tsx, which carried ₹10/month in the meta description, the Open Graph card, the Twitter card and the schema.org offer price. That is the text Google indexes and the text that unfurls when anyone shares sellos.in in WhatsApp — so a chemist could tap a ₹799 ad, forward the link to their brother, and have the preview quote ₹10. All four now read ₹799.

One gap left, and it is a decision not a bug

The flyer and field kit sell three terms — ₹799/month, ₹3,999 for six, ₹6,999 for the year. sellos.in sells one: ₹799/month. Nothing contradicts, but a chemist who takes a flyer and then checks the site cannot find the annual price the founder just quoted them. Either add the two prepaid tiers to the landing pricing section, or drop them from the print assets and keep prepaid as a closing lever used in person only. The ad itself is unaffected either way — it quotes ₹799/month, which is true everywhere.
