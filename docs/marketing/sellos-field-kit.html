Field Kit

Everything the person walking the shops needs to say, skip, and write down. No ads, no channel partners, no software to build. Doors and a phone.

Goal — two paying shops
Sheet 01

What it costs

The ₹4,999 six-month price was broken. It works out to ₹833/month — ₹34 more than paying monthly. Anyone who does the arithmetic stops trusting the rest of the sheet. Corrected ladder below: every step down is a real saving.

Monthly ₹799 per month First month free
6 months ₹3,999 ₹666 / month Save ₹795 · 17% off
1 year ₹6,999 ₹583 / month Save ₹2,589 · 27% off

How the free month works on each plan

  • Monthly: month one free, first ₹799 charged at the start of month two.
  • Prepaid: add the free month to the term instead of refunding it — 7 months for ₹3,999, 13 months for ₹6,999. Same promise, no refund to argue about later.
  • Setup is done inside week one on every plan. That is the promise you are actually selling.

On "testing which price works"

With five shops there is no statistical signal — one chatty owner swings the whole result. So don't split shops into price A and price B. Put all three on one sheet, let the owner pick, and record which they chose and the exact words they used about money. That is real learning at this sample size. Conversion rates are not.

Sheet 02

Who to walk into, who to walk past

Pitch

All of these true, judged from the doorway in ten seconds:

  • One billing counter, two at most.
  • Roughly 200–800 medicines. Count racks — one rack is about 150–250.
  • Bills written in a copy, or software they bought once and stopped using.
  • Owner or owner's family is standing there and decides.
  • A smartphone at the counter with 4G that actually works.
Walk past

Any one of these and you leave politely. Saying so out loud wins more trust than pitching:

  • Running Marg or Vasy every day and content with it. Not your fight yet.
  • Three or more billing counters.
  • Wants distributor invoices imported automatically. You don't have it.
  • Bills by barcode scanner. You don't have it.
  • Internet drops most days. You have no offline mode — don't pretend otherwise.
  • Owner is out. Book a time and come back. Never pitch the staff.
Sheet 03

Where to walk — the second ring

The three chemists at the hospital gate are the obvious targets and the wrong ones. They do the highest volume, so they are the most likely to already run software, they are the most guarded about their numbers, and they compete hardest with each other.

Start 300 metres to 1 kilometre out — the para chemists serving residents rather than discharge queues. Same walkable density, far higher hit rate.

  • Work one road at a time. Neighbours talk. Six shops on one stretch beats six shops across the city.
  • Go between 2 and 4 in the afternoon. Morning and evening are their rush; you will be waved off.
  • Never on Saturday. Busiest day of the week. Good for watching how they work, useless for talking.
  • Fifteen doors a day. Not thirty. A rushed conversation teaches you nothing.
Sheet 04 · in order, every time

The three-minute doorstep

Deliver it in Bengali or Hindi as comes naturally. The lines below are the ones worth getting exactly right. Say them, then stop talking.

  1. Open with the notebook, not the software

    15 sec

    The word "software" ends the conversation. The copy on the counter starts it.

    ENDo you still write your bills in a copy?
    BNআপনার বিল কি এখনো খাতায় লেখেন?
    HIक्या आप अभी भी बिल कॉपी में लिखते हैं?

  2. Ask three questions and listen

    30 sec

    Do not pitch here. You are finding out which of the three pains is his pain, because that is the one you demo.

    • How do you know which medicines are expiring next month?
    • When your CA asks for the GST figures, how long does that take you?
    • When a customer returns a strip, how do you work out what to give back?

  3. Show one bill on your phone

    60 sec

    Phone out, demo shop already logged in. Make a real bill in front of him and say the seconds out loud. Then show the thing he complained about in step two — the expiry warning, the GST report, or the return. Then the PDF bill with the drug licence on it. Nothing else.

  4. Remove the wall

    30 sec

    Every shop that "will start next month" is stuck on typing in their stock. This line is the whole offer — the software is what he keeps afterwards.

    ENYou don't have to type any of it. We come to the shop and enter your whole stock ourselves. Free.
    BNআপনাকে কিছু টাইপ করতে হবে না — আমরা এসে আপনার পুরো স্টক ফ্রি-তে তুলে দেব।
    HIआपको कुछ टाइप नहीं करना है — हम आकर आपका पूरा स्टॉक मुफ़्त में डाल देंगे।

  5. Ask for a time, not for the sale

    15 sec

    Closing at the counter is not the job. A booked slot with a phone number is. "Can I come Tuesday at two, and we'll set it up together?" Then take the WhatsApp number and write the date down in front of him.

    Don't quote a price unless he asks. If he asks: first month free, then ₹799 a month, or ₹6,999 for the year which is ₹583. Then move to the next shop.

Sheet 05

What they'll say back

He saysYou say
"Marg is one payment. Yours is every month, forever." Marg is ten to twelve thousand up front, plus the yearly maintenance, plus a computer, plus paying someone to install it. We're nothing today and ₹583 a month if you take the year. And we come and enter your stock — nobody else does that part.
"And if the internet goes?" Use your phone's hotspot, it's the same app. Be straight: if your net goes down most days, don't take this yet — I'll come back when you've sorted the connection.
"My boy can't work a computer." It runs on the phone he's already holding. Search the medicine, put the quantity, done. Hand him your phone and let him make the bill. Don't describe it.
"The shop next door will see my sales." Every shop gets its own separate database. There's no shared list — we couldn't mix them if we wanted to.
"Who are you? You'll shut down in six months." We built this for a chemist in [area], they've been billing on it every day since [month]. Here's the owner's number — call him. (Ask that owner's permission first, once.)
"Does it pull my distributor's bill automatically?" No. You enter the purchase once when the stock arrives. If auto-import is the thing you need, we're not the right fit yet — I'd rather tell you now.
"Leave your card, I'll think about it." Don't push. "Should I come back next week, or after the pujo?" Get a date, write it down, and actually turn up on it.

The three honest "we're not right for you" answers above are not lost sales. They are the reason the shop believes the rest of what you said — and half of them call back when their situation changes.

Sheet 06 · the promise you sold

Getting a shop live, mostly without going there

Do not send them a blank spreadsheet

A 400-product shop holds roughly 700 batches. Each one needs product, company, HSN, batch number, expiry, MRP, buying price, selling price, quantity, box, GST rate, distributor and invoice number — about 8,400 cells. No shop owner in his fifties is filling that, and the ones who promise to won't. A blank template is where onboarding goes to die, and you'll spend three weeks chasing a file that never comes.

Ask for documents that already exist instead

Every batch on his shelf arrived on a piece of paper he still has. Work down this list and stop at the first one that applies:

  • He runs software already, even abandoned. Marg and every competitor export an item and stock master to Excel. One file, five minutes of his time, and you have the whole shop. Always ask this first — a dead Marg licence is a gift.
  • He has distributor purchase bills. Ask him to WhatsApp photos of the last 60–90 days. Those bills already carry product, batch number, expiry, MRP, buying price, quantity, distributor and invoice number — nearly the whole schema. One invoice photo is 20–50 batches, and it costs him a minute.
  • Neither — paper khata only. Only now does the template come out, and only for the fast-movers below. Send it pre-filled with his top products already typed in, so he's correcting rather than authoring.

Enter the fast-movers only. Not the shop.

The top 100–150 SKUs are roughly 80% of what he bills in a day. Enter those and he can go live. The long tail loads itself — every distributor delivery from then on adds its own batches as he receives them, so the back rack fills in over a month without anyone doing a migration.

This is the difference between a three-day migration and an afternoon, and it's the single biggest lever on how many shops you can onboard in a fortnight.

Chemists are the right first market partly because they almost all keep a computer and printer at the counter — drug licence record-keeping sees to that. A 30-minute screen share does what a visit used to, and they're already used to a "software wala" remote-connecting to fix things.

The 48-hour rule

Remote costs you completion. A shop that says "I'll send the bills tonight" and doesn't is the main way a won deal quietly dies, and no amount of WhatsApp fixes it. Nothing received within 48 hours of the yes — get in the car. Presence forces completion in a way messaging never will. Also go in person when the training call clearly didn't land, or when the shop is big enough to be worth a case study.

Day 0

Yes, then a group

WhatsApp group: owner, you, field person. Ask for the export or the bill photos in that first message, while he's still enthusiastic. Everything about this shop lives in that group. No email.

Day 1

Shop identity + fast-movers in

GSTIN, drug and food licences, address, logo configured so his very first bill already looks like his shop. Then the top 100–150 SKUs typed in from whatever he sent.

Day 2

Training call — 30 minutes

Screen share. He makes a real bill for a real customer while you watch. If you make it for him he never learns and he churns in month two.

Day 3–14

Shadow

Answer the group within minutes. No bills for three days running: phone him. Still nothing the next day: go there. A silent shop is a churning shop, and WhatsApp will not save it.

Sheet 07

What gets written down

One WhatsApp message from the field person at the end of each day, straight into a Google Sheet. Do not build a CRM. Do not buy one.

  • Shops walked into · shop name and road
  • Owner present? yes / no
  • Copy, or software — and which one
  • Demo actually shown? yes / no
  • Callback booked, with the date
  • The objection he heard most today, in the owner's own words
  • WhatsApp numbers collected

Paying the field person

Weight it towards outcomes, not doors: a small daily for showing up, roughly ₹300 for every demo the owner actually watched, and ₹2,000 when a shop pays. Adjust the numbers to what you've agreed — the shape is what matters, because paying per door gets you thirty rushed doors and no demos.

Sheet 08

Six weeks, then the gate

Week 1–2

Learn the pitch on real doors

75 shops walked. Expect around 12 real demos and 5 booked callbacks. You are hunting objections, not sales. Rewrite Sheet 05 at the end of week one with what you actually heard.

Week 2

Ask the Kolkata shop for names

Not "tell people about us" — five names and numbers. He won't refer the shop across the road, so ask specifically for chemists in other paras, or the ones he chats to at the distributor's counter.

Week 3–4

Convert the callbacks

Work the booked slots and the referrals. Two shops signed and set up is the target. Founder does the closes; field person does the doors.

Week 5–6

Make them stick

Both shops billing daily, unaided. Write the one-page case study: bills per day, seconds per bill, expiry losses caught, hours saved at GST time. Real numbers from their dashboard.

The gate

Do not approach distributor sales reps, the BCDA units, or CAs until two shops have been paying and billing every day for thirty days.

Those are leverage channels — they multiply a pitch across dozens of shops at once. Hand them an untested pitch and you burn a channel you cannot buy back, and there is no ad budget to fall back on. Past the gate, the rep channel is commission-only at around ₹1,500 a close, and one rep covers more doors in a week than your field person covers in a month.
